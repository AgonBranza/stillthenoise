who he is

Agon Branza is a Lisbon born, London based, award winning singer songwriter and composer whose work draws from classical music, confessional songwriting and avant garde electronic production, shaped by his studies in cinema and music for film. He completed his conservatoire training in music theory and piano, and was the frontman of the band Soapbox, winner of "Best New Alternative Act" at the Rock Rendez Worten competition and selected to play the Super Bock Super Rock festival alongside Depeche Mode and Nouvelle Vague.

As a composer, working under his given name Gonçalo Abrantes, his output includes a five piece piano cycle, Inner Anthems, streamed more than 40,000 times and featured across three of Nils Frahm's Piano Day playlists. His scoring work spans advertising and numerous award winning independent films, including a Microsoft campaign that won his team a Silver Lion at the Cannes Festival, and Best Soundtrack at the Prémios Lusófonos de Criatividade for a Vitalis water Olympics campaign.

As a producer, his credits include Steinsdotter (#3 on the UK Club & Commercial Charts), Heren Wolf, Ana de Llor (DrakeYolanda award, 2022), Amelie Jat, Faultress and Tsinder Ash, several of which have been played on BBC Radio 6.

As a performer, he has performed and/or recorded for artists including Alanis Morissette, Patrick Watson, Jools Holland, Nitin Sawhney, Emeli Sandé, Guy Garvey of Elbow, Rick Astley, Kelly Lee Owens and the BBC Orchestra, and has performed at the Royal Albert Hall, Abbey Road Studio One, the Barbican, among many other major venues in London.

the idea

most breakup albums go straight for blame.
this one doesn't.

it's about accountability instead, the harder and quieter thing. it moves through four stages: questioning, accountability, acceptance, and impermanence.

it opens and closes on the same wall of noise. the first time, in "before language," it's the noise of a conflict already happening, the struggle to react, to find the right words while it's unfolding. by the end, that exact same noise becomes release. it never really resolves, because these things never do. it stays with you, never finished, just worked through as best you can. that's where the title comes from: the noise is always there, in one form or another, looping. "still, the noise," the title track, is the emotional core the rest of the album orbits.

most of the lyrics arrived in a single night, after months of sitting on all of it. it just had to come out. that's the moment he knew this had to be an album, not just songs.

the music is eclectic, it moves around, but the lyrics are what hold it together: heartbreak written from an accountability and self aware perspective. he never stopped asking what went wrong. what changed was also asking what his part in it was. every story has two sides, and this one tries to hold both equally, instead of the usual pointing finger breakup record.

the album comes from a queer perspective, and it's who he expects it to speak to most directly, even where the songs don't name it outright. that's part of what shapes the accountability the record keeps circling back to: not one side at fault, but two people, in a story that isn't always told from this angle.

the website  ·  stillthenoise.com

the album, taken apart
and handed back.

the album has a home: stillthenoise.com. it started because when he played early demos to friends, before the melody, before anything else, they went straight for the words, some of them cried. that reaction is what shaped the whole project.

instead of a normal rollout, the site turns the album into something people can actually enter and make their own: six tools built from the record's language, a triptych generator, a magnetic poetry board, a hidden word game, an unsent letter builder, a set of confessional questions, and the Silent Rooms, interactive lyric experiences built as an alternative to the music video, one per song, each its own small world, meant to bring people closer to the words while they're actually listening, the way a physical CD booklet once did. what people build inside them collects in a shared space called the holding room. right now the triptych and magnetic poetry creations can be printed, turning a private, half anonymous act of making something out of someone else's words into a physical object a person can hold. more of the tools will get that same treatment over time.

collaborators

All three featured artists on the album, Meg Ella Brookes, ILĀ and Heren Wolf, are connected through Agon's years with London Contemporary Voices, the choir behind performances with Grammy winning artists including Florence + The Machine and U2. Meg Ella is a Welsh singer-songwriter, cellist and pianist whose debut EP was praised by BBC Radio 3 and championed personally by Jools Holland. ILĀ, who both sings on and co-produces "as if life," is an artist and producer whose own work sits at the intersection of voice, AI and quantum computing, presented at Harvard, Oxford and the World Economic Forum, and made in collaboration with names like Imogen Heap, Guy Sigsworth, Andrew Bird, Florence + The Machine and U2. Heren Wolf, also from the choir, is a London-based singer-songwriter in his own right.

The album is mixed by George Papadopoulos (Journeyman), whose credits include immersive and Atmos mixing work for The Chemical Brothers, the LA Philharmonic, the Barbie soundtrack, David Guetta, Sub Focus, MUNA and Sam Ryder, and mastered by Oli Morgan, whose credits include Bastille, Ólafur Arnalds, Gregory Porter, Seal and The Beatles: Now and Then.

where it's headed

this was never going to be a normal release,
so i'm not treating it like one.

right now i'm figuring out where it belongs, and who it belongs in front of. less playlists and radio, more the places this kind of thing actually lives.

01
literary worlds

readings, small presses, poetry communities. the words live there as easily as they live in song.

02
a room you can enter

a small exhibition or installation built from the album's words, and the things people make from them.

03
the words, given back

people's fragments projected at live shows. their grief, their writing, up on the wall. given back, and seen.

less about the usual machine, more about the right rooms and the right people.

the launch  ·  early 2027

a full live performance of the
whole record, start to finish.

played by a full band: string quartet, backing vocalists, piano, live electronics and drums. the room itself will carry the website into it, a printing station on site and the website's games available to play, so the fragments people make are given back to them in the same night. it's the part he's most excited about: the whole thing in one room, everything the site does made real.
